# Requirements and open interpretations

## Course requirements

- Eight students; every member contributes technical work and speaks in the final presentation.
- Every member develops at least 2-3 model architectures and submits their work to the leaderboard, according to the supplied assignment.
- Establish an official baseline; compare at least two meaningful strategies under consistent evaluation; quantify major changes and analyze at least one genuine failed/ineffective approach.
- Make multiple submissions over the project period and preserve baseline, best score, history and course-cutoff standing.
- One combined academic report (IEEE or APA), one presentation and an actual contribution table.
- With split Kaggle teams, include both names, memberships, baseline/best scores and submission histories.
- Each member submits a peer review of the other seven.

## Grading

G.1 setup: 5; G.2 processing/baseline: 5; G.3 experiments: 5; G.4 submissions/tracking: 5; G.5 analysis/presentation: 5; G.6 comparative standing and baseline improvement: 10. Total: 35.

## Planning choices

One shared repository; two team directories; two small architectures per person; Days 3-5 individual uploads; Day 6 repair/evaluation buffer; Day 7 verification/rehearsal. These are recommendations, not additional course mandates.

## Confirm on Day 1

- Exact course cutoff, deliverable deadlines and presentation duration.
- Whether two or three architectures are expected; accepted model families; whether each variant requires a separate official submission.
- How two Kaggle sub-team results combine for grading.
- Current team/sharing rules, daily upload quota/reset, active-agent behavior, runtime resources and required package format.
- Usable runtime, replay availability and measured simulator throughput.

The TA announcements specify separate Kaggle teams when required by team-size limits; they do not specify the number of GitHub repositories. Do not represent the shared repository as organizer permission for cross-team code sharing.
